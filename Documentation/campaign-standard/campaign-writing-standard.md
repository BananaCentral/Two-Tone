# Campaign Writing Standard

The Dylan chapter excerpt is the style reference. Two-Tone follows Soul in close third person and past tense. Stay with what he sees, hears, thinks, and does. Keep the voice casual even when a scene leads into a fight.

## Write the moment in order

- Show what catches Soul’s attention, how he reacts, and what he does next. A sound wakes him. He waits to hear it again. When a branch snaps, he reaches for his sword.
- Use plain, concrete language and everyday actions. Soul wipes slime off his blade or rolls the tent a second time.
- Slow down for details that reveal something about Soul, affect his decisions, or set up a later moment. Move quickly through routine actions once the reader understands them.
- Carry practical details across beats. Soul leaves his sword on the riverbank before reaching for it during the ambush. The wolves come to him while he is still at camp.

## Use short paragraphs and casual reactions

- Start a paragraph when Soul’s attention shifts, he begins a new action, or something interrupts him. Mix brief reactions with slightly longer paragraphs.
- Let Soul notice ordinary things. Seeing rooftops makes him think about breakfast. Finding slime on his clothes makes him stop to wash.
- Let humor come from Soul’s reactions and the timing of interruptions. A simple “Seriously?” can carry the moment. Give quieter scenes room to stay quiet.

## Write for the campaign screen

- Give each paragraph its own story beat and Continue screen. This includes standalone reactions, lines of dialogue, and punchlines. Let text wrap within the screen. Repeat the portrait and any background override when they carry across consecutive beats.
- Check that each beat fits beside its portrait and above the controls. Split a crowded beat at a natural pause.
- Choose a portrait for the reaction the player should notice. Let the artwork show the expression when the text already makes the feeling clear.
- End a pre-battle scene as the encounter begins, then show **Begin Battle** and let the player take over. Store wave counts, enemy levels, and rewards in the encounter setup.

## Example

> Beyond the low hills, he could just make out the rooftops of a village.
>
> His expression brightened. If he got there early enough, he might be able to find somewhere that served breakfast.
>
> As the sun came up over the hills, a low growl drew his attention back to the campsite.
>
> Three wolves stepped out of the bushes beside the cold fire pit. Soul stood there with one hand still on his pack strap.
>
> He hadn’t even started walking yet.

The village gives Soul somewhere to go. Breakfast makes the reason his own, and the last line lets the interruption land before the fight.
